# Solar geometry and the sun-elevation frame

**Decided:** `lastlight.geometry` holds the one conversion between clock time and sun elevation: `solar_position` (NOAA series, standard library only), `ELEVATION_SWEEP` and `ELEVATION_BANDS`, `elevation_crossings`, `sunset_azimuth` and `transect` (issue #4). pvlib's SPA implementation is a dev-only dependency and the test oracle.

- **The oracle is pvlib's geometric `elevation`, never `apparent_elevation`.** A test pins the difference: at geometric zero, pvlib's apparent elevation is about half a degree higher, and ours must follow the geometric column.
- **Measured against SPA** (every hour of every 7th day of 2026, 1272 instants): worst elevation error 0.0140 degrees (limit 0.02), worst azimuth error 0.0315 degrees over the 732 instants with the sun above -10 degrees (limit 0.05). The worst elevation errors are all in February: that is the NOAA series itself, not a bug here. The margin is real, so the limits cannot be halved without changing the method.
- **The evening is defined from longitude alone.** `elevation_crossings(date, lat, lon)` takes no timezone, so "the evening of the local date" means the evening after local mean solar noon on that date. The setting sun is searched in a window of 30 minutes to 11.5 hours after mean noon, which sits entirely after true noon and before true midnight (they differ from the mean by under 17 minutes), so the elevation falls monotonically and bisection is valid. The instants usually land on the next UTC day; a test checks they fall on the right local date and that month and year ends roll over.
- **Azimuth is an `atan2` of the east and north components,** the same quantity as NOAA's `acos` form, without its branch cut and without a division by zero at the zenith.
- **A naive datetime is refused.** Reading it as local time would silently move the sun by hours.
- **`ELEVATION_BANDS` is a plain dict** so it can go straight into canonical JSON for the seal. A mapping proxy would not serialise.
- **The equation of time is a named private function** so an in-suite test can replace it with its negation and show the pvlib comparison going red (the teeth mutation, by decisive margins: errors in degrees against limits in hundredths).
- **Test locations are derived from the public reference point** (mirrored latitude, shifted longitude, a longitude beside the antimeridian) so no other site coordinates are written down.

**Rejected alternatives:** vectorised numpy (breaks the no-runtime-dependency rule, and an evening needs only a few hundred scalar calls); adding the parallax correction (0.002 degrees, below the series' own error, and the issue asks for NOAA); an `acos` azimuth with clamping (works, but needs a zenith special case); returning the high end of the bisection bracket instead of its midpoint (doubles the worst-case timing error for no gain).

**What broke:**

- `ruff check` sorted the `lastlight` imports differently when given a single file path than when run over the project, so the import block flip-flopped. The gate runs project-wide; only trust that run.
- The first teeth run's equation-of-time mutant pattern no longer matched once `ruff format` had reflowed the line. The script reports unmatched patterns instead of skipping them, which is how it was caught; the mutant was then rerun.
