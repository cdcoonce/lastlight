# Renderer v0: single scattering in a spherical shell

**Decided:** `lastlight.render` paints the western twilight sky from an `Atmosphere` with a single-scattering model in a spherical-shell atmosphere, Earth's shadow included (issue #7). `render_curve` returns the CIELAB colour for every sun elevation from +6 to -8 degrees and seven view elevations, in about one second.

- **Shells, not points.** The atmosphere is 131 concentric shells (50 m near the ground, 1 km above 5 km). Every shell carries the *mean* density of the real profile, so the optical depth of any straight path is the sum of path length in each shell times the shell's extinction. The path length in a shell is the difference of two ray-sphere chords, which is exact for any direction, including the grazing sunward paths of deep twilight.
- **Earth's shadow is one ray-sphere test.** A scattering point is lit if the straight path from it toward the sun does not cross the Earth (the discriminant of the ray-sphere intersection). Unlit points contribute zero. That is the whole of the twilight mechanism; there is no special case.
- **Independent check.** The tests compare the shell integrator with a brute-force Riemann sum of the same physics (4000 steps along the view, 600 along each sun path, densities evaluated point by point). They agree within 0.9 percent at every geometry tried (9 geometries from sun +20 to -8, 5 wavelengths), for two atmospheres, one with every parameter off its default. The plane-parallel closed form holds to 0.12 percent at high sun.
- **Exposure convention (recorded, as the issue asks).** Each sun elevation is exposed on its own so its brightest view elevation reads L* = 70. The curve then says how colour varies along the sky at one moment, not how fast the evening darkens. The camera will be normalised the same way, per sun elevation, before the two are compared.
- **Luminance scale.** Y = 1 is a white card lit by the extraterrestrial sun at normal incidence. Colours are read against a D65 white with no chromatic adaptation, so the sun itself is slightly warm.
- **Data.** The ozone absorption table and the extraterrestrial solar spectrum are the visible rows of the SPCTRL2 coefficient table (Bird and Riordan 1986), copied row for row from pvlib's BSD-3-Clause copy; licence text and provenance are in `src/lastlight/render/data/README.md`. Ozone sits in a Gaussian layer centred at 22 km with a standard deviation of 6 km.
- **Rayleigh.** The Rayleigh formula with Peck and Reeder's refractive index and the Bodhaine King factor gives a vertical optical depth of 0.0969 at 550 nm and 1013.25 hPa, 0.4 percent under Bucholtz's 0.0973. It is Bucholtz's recipe, not a transcription of his table: I did not have the table to hand and will not type numbers from memory.
- **colour-science without SciPy.** It supplies the colour-matching functions, the D65 point and the XYZ to CIELAB and sRGB maths. It warns at import that SciPy and Matplotlib are missing; neither is needed, so the import is wrapped to keep the notices out of the test and CLI output.
- **The sweep is defined here for now.** `geometry.ELEVATION_SWEEP` (issue #4) is not on main, and creating `geometry.py` in this PR would collide with that slice. `render.curve.SUN_ELEVATIONS` repeats the definition; a test compares the two and skips until `lastlight.geometry` exists. When it lands, import the constant and keep the test.

**Rejected alternatives:**

- A 5772 K blackbody for the sun: simpler, and wrong by ten percent across the band, with no Fraunhofer structure. The table was already being bundled.
- A higher-resolution ozone cross-section (Serdyuchenko, Bass and Paur, Brion-Daumont-Malicet): the redistribution terms are unclear, or the only convenient copy is inside GPL code. SPCTRL2's coarse table is enough for a smooth, broad Chappuis band.
- Point sampling the view and sun paths on a uniform grid: needs thousands of steps per path to resolve a 1.5 km aerosol layer next to a 1000 km grazing path. Kept as the test oracle only.
- SciPy for quadrature or interpolation: not needed, and it is a large install on every CI run.

**What broke:**

- **Acceptance criterion 2 is false with haze, along the sun's azimuth.** With the issue's aerosol (g = 0.7, single-scatter albedo 0.93) and AOD 0.05, the 2-degree view at sun +20 has chroma 17.1 and the 45-degree view 5.1: the low view is only 18 degrees from the sun, inside the forward-scattering aureole, and the sky above it is whitened by the same haze. It holds at AOD 0 (7.0 against 16.5) and at AOD 0.1 away from the sun (13.5 against 14.2 at 90 degrees, 9.4 against 15.9 at 135). The tests assert it in clean air along the sun's azimuth and with haze away from the sun, and add the aureole as its own sign test.
- **Acceptance criterion 3 contradicts itself.** "Redder (higher a*) and lowers its b* toward yellow-orange" cannot both hold: yellow-orange has high b*. The model gives a* from 11 to 29 and b* flat (47 to 49) for AOD 0.05 to 0.5 at sun +2, 5-degree view. The hue angle falls from 77 to 60 degrees, yellow turning orange, and that is what the test asserts, with a* rising. Hue angle has the advantage that the per-sun-elevation exposure does not change it; a* and b* both do.
- **Ruff classes `lastlight.render` as third-party while the package does not exist,** so the red test commit has an import-order finding that the green commit resolves.

**Known limits of v0, stated so nobody mistakes them for results:** single scattering only, so the sky at sun -6 to -8 degrees is too dark where multiple scattering would lift it; no ground reflection; no refraction; no water vapour or oxygen bands; the spectral tables are 10 to 40 nm apart. Bruneton's table (M4) replaces all of this through the same interface.
