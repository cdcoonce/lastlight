# Renderer v1 against the Prague Sky Model at sun 0, and the forward-lobe attribution (#45)

**Result:** the two rules pre-registered in `docs/renderer/prague-sun0-criterion.md` (landed in commit fabbbdc, before any sun-0 number existed) were implemented as written and run on the committed fixtures. **Rule A, which side of the `aod_550` 0.1 gap Prague is on: cannot tell, branch 1.** D is -0.0337, E is +0.0445, s is 0.0269, m is 0.0247, so U is 0.0269 and 2U is 0.0538; E is not above 2U. The criterion predicted this outcome; it says the comparison lacks power, not anything about either model. **Rule B, what the toward-sun luminance excess is made of: aerosol lobe, branch 2.** Fitted at sun +10, the Henyey-Greenstein asymmetry is 0.67 at `aod_550` 0.03 and 0.60 at 0.1 (v1 has 0.70). Applied at sun 0, where nobody had computed anything, it cuts the root mean square excess r over the six sunward nominal views from 0.1253 to 0.0560 at 0.03 (0.447 of the excess is left; the bar is 0.5) and from 0.4322 to 0.1785 at 0.1 (0.413 is left). Both conditions count (r at 0.7 is above the 0.05 floor), so the test is made on both. The port is excluded as the main cause by its bound alone: B is 0.0105 and 0.0238, so 2B is 0.0209 and 0.0476, both under the floor. Renderer v1 is unchanged. A change to its aerosol lobe would be a new renderer with its own registration, which the conductor files. The tool reproduced every sun +10 pilot number the criterion quotes (table below). Two readings of the result, both chosen after the first look and so exploratory, are recorded under "What the numbers say beyond the rules": the lobe verdict's margin at 0.03 is thin and reading-dependent (it holds under Prague's primary reading, which the verdict is defined on, and under none of the four other readings, where r(g*) / r(0.7) is 0.501 to 0.964 against the 0.5 bar; rule B, unlike rule A, had no term for the reading), and at sun 0 v1 is brighter than Prague over the whole sky, by more away from the sun than toward it, which a lower asymmetry does not touch.

## What was run, and how to reproduce it

- **Rules in the tool, tests first.** `tools/prague_reference.py` gained the rules as pure functions (`rule_a_verdict`, `rule_a`, `best_asymmetry_k`, `excess`, `rule_b`, `port_bound`) with every registered constant named. `tests/test_prague_sun0.py` tests every branch and every boundary the criterion states on synthetic inputs. That file's first commit is red (the tool had none of it); the implementation is the next commit. No Prague number at sun 0 existed at either.
- **The lobe diagnostic** (`lobe_xyz`, in the tool only, never in `lastlight.render`) is the criterion's two calls: v0's single scattering at `Atmosphere(aod_550=a, asymmetry=g)` plus `multiple_scattering_xyz(Atmosphere(aod_550=a), ...)`. v1 is never called with an asymmetry. At g = 0.7 it equals v1's XYZ exactly (`np.array_equal`), pinned at sun +10 and 0, both loads, relative azimuths 0, 30, 90 and 180.
- **The fixture builder** takes the sun elevation, the azimuth list and the output path (`FixtureSpec`). With its defaults it regenerates `tests/fixtures/prague/sun10.json` byte for byte: checked by regenerating to a scratch path with `--output` and comparing with `cmp`, and by the network-marked `fixture --check`. It refuses a sun elevation outside the dataset's sun axis (-4.2 to 90 degrees) and any Prague luminance that is not finite and above 0, naming the point.
- **The fixture** `tests/fixtures/prague/sun0.json` was written after the rules and the lobe engine were committed: `uv run python tools/prague_reference.py fixture --sun 0` (about 30 seconds here, the model and datasets already in `~/.cache/lastlight/prague/`). Relative azimuths 0, 30, 90 and 180, the same eight views and two loads as `sun10.json`, every diagnostic reading `sun10.json` has, its sun elevation recorded. The same commit, dataset digest and visibility mapping as `sun10.json`; the mapped visibilities (123.82 and 49.99 km) and the clamped matched reading at 0.03 (131.8 km) are the same too.
- **The numbers** are `uv run python tools/prague_reference.py report --sun 0`, from the two committed fixtures alone (the fit is `sun10.json`'s, the ruling `sun0.json`'s; about 5 seconds). `report --sun 0` ends with rule B run under each other reading of Prague (see "What the numbers say beyond the rules"). `report` (no argument) is #34's report and is byte-identical to before; it now refuses the sun-0 fixture, so #34's `aod_550` 0.1 verdict is not printed for sun 0.
- **Checks:** `fixture --sun 0 --check` and `fixture --check` (the network-marked tests) both pass; the full suite passes: 3506 tests before the review round (3 minutes 12 seconds in one run on a quiet host) and 3521 after it (run in four chunks by path under a load average above 200); 8 network-marked tests are deselected in both.

## What the numbers say beyond the rules

Exploratory: chosen after the first look at the numbers above, so not evidence for either side. Every figure here is printed by `report --sun 0` under its "Exploratory" heading.

- **D has the wrong sign for both hypotheses.** The port predicts 0 and the Monte Carlo +0.0445; D is -0.0337, and it was +0.0326 at sun +10 over the same points. It is nearer the port (|D| 0.0337) than the Monte Carlo (|D - E| 0.0782), and the alternative readings do not rescue either: -0.0441 and -0.0584 (the two that make m), -0.0243 (binned), -0.0061 (observer at altitude 0). Had U been small enough to clear branch 1 (under 0.0223), rule A would have fallen to branch 4, because |D| and |D - E| are both larger than that U. So more power alone would not have turned "cannot tell" into "port" or "Monte Carlo"; a reference with v1's own aerosol is what could.
- **The gap's own point cannot speak.** At azimuth 30, view 1.51, d is +0.111 against e +0.048; the point sits 30 degrees from the sun, where the toward-sun excess dominates d (mean d at azimuth 30 is +0.112, at azimuth 0 +0.294).
- **The lobe verdict's margin is thin at 0.03.** The bar is 0.5 and the ratio is 0.447 (r of 0.0560 against a bar of 0.0627); at 0.1 it is 0.413. The sun +10 fit transfers exactly at 0.03 (0.67 at both suns: the grid value that minimises r at sun 0 alone is also 0.67) and does not at 0.1 (0.60 fitted at sun +10, 0.48 at sun 0 alone). After the fit the 0.03 bias is gone (signed mean -0.0012) and what is left is a slope over the six views, from +0.097 at the lowest to -0.067 at the reference band. At 0.1 the fit removes 59 percent of the rms excess and leaves v1 too bright toward the sun by a mean log ratio of +0.1701, a factor of 1.19.
- **The lobe verdict is reading-dependent: it holds under the registered reading of Prague and under none of the other four at 0.03.** Rule B was registered on Prague's primary reading and has no term for the reading; rule A has one (m). Reported, not ruled, and with the registered verdict unchanged: rule B was run end to end under each of the fixture's four other readings of the same model (g*(a) refitted at sun +10 under the reading, then r at 0.7, r at g*(a), their ratio and the count at sun 0 under it; the table is in `report --sun 0` under "Rule B under the other readings of Prague"). The bar is a ratio of 0.5.

## Decisions, each with its rejected alternative

- **The rules are pure functions of numbers, and the verdict is a function of D, E and U alone** (`rule_a_verdict`). Every boundary is then tested on exactly representable values: E exactly 2U, |D| exactly U, |D - E| exactly U, r(g*) exactly half of r(0.7), r(0.7) exactly 0.05, one load under the floor while the other halves, and the grid fit's two tie-breaks. Rejected: running the rules only through the fixtures (the real numbers never land on a bound, so no boundary would be tested).
- **Commit order is evidence.** Red rule tests, rule code, red engine tests, engine, fixture builder, fixture, run and pins, exploratory figures. Rejected: one commit, or fixture first: the rules' bars would then have a number in view.
- **The diagnostic lives in the tool.** `lobe_xyz` composes v0 and the table; there is no `asymmetry` parameter on v1 and nothing under `src/` changed. Rejected: letting v1 take an asymmetry (v1's physics is pre-registered, definition 8 of `docs/gates/renderer-v1/criterion.md`).
- **The sun elevation is an argument everywhere**: `engine_xyz`, `predicted_if_monte_carlo`, e_i, the lobe engine and the #34 helpers read it from the fixture, never from `SUN_ELEVATION_DEG`. The #34 `report` now reads the fixture's sun and refuses any but +10. Rejected: keeping the module constant (a cold read found these hard-wired to +10: a test pins E over P at sun 0 to 0.044527 from v1 alone and a second to +0.0341 at sun +10).
- **Points are selected by value.** Rule A's P is "azimuth in {90, 180}", the nominal views are "the elevations of `build_views()["nominal"]`, each found exactly once to 1e-9", the gap's point is "azimuth 30 and the view that rounds to 1.51". A test runs both rules on a copy of each fixture with its azimuths and views listed in the opposite order. Rejected: the existing `report`'s mask of `[False, True, True]` repeated, which would pick the wrong azimuths in a four-azimuth fixture.
- **A registry of two fixtures, `--sun` and `--output`.** `fixture --sun 0` and `report --sun 0`; `--output` moves where a registered fixture is written or checked; the recorded `provenance.command` is the registered one either way. Rejected: a free `--sun` with default azimuths (a fixture with no registered home) and an `--azimuths` option (the recorded command could no longer remake the file).
- **sun10.json is proven unchanged by regenerating it to a scratch path and comparing bytes.** Rejected: relying on `fixture --check`, whose tolerance is 1e-6 relative.
- **Pins are by value with `pytest.approx(x, rel=1e-6, abs=0)`**, plus the exact verdict strings. Rejected: a hash of float output, and pytest's default absolute tolerance, which would void a relative bar on values this small.
- **Each verdict is reached on a stand-in for Prague** (a fixture whose Prague is the port, is the Monte Carlo, is neither, is v1, is the fitted lobe, or is v1 scaled up), so the pipeline is shown able to say each thing and not only "cannot tell" and "aerosol lobe".
- **The report prints the sun +10 rule A statistics but no verdict for them.** Rejected: a verdict on the pilot data, which would read as a second ruling.
- **Rule B under the other readings is new code beside `evaluate_rule_b`, reported and not ruled.** The registered function, its constants and its verdict are untouched, and the sweep run under the primary reading reproduces the ruling (a test pins that). Rejected: a reading parameter on `evaluate_rule_b`, or folding the readings into the verdict (that would be a second ruling, or a change to a pre-registered rule after the numbers were seen).
- **The exploratory figures are in the tool's report under a heading that says what they are**, not hand-computed for this log. Rejected: quoting numbers whose instrument is not committed.

## What broke, and what surprised

- **One of my red tests was wrong, not the rule.** The test of s's degrees of freedom built its alternative readings with four points against a two-point d; the tool rightly refused. It was fixed in the implementation commit.
- **A mutation pass found three weak spots.** Squaring before averaging was untested (the first test used logarithms of equal size, where the root mean square equals the mean absolute value), the rule B guard against fixtures at the wrong suns had no test, and the report's per-azimuth rows were not pinned. Tests for all three were added and the mutants re-killed. One round of apparent survivors was my mutation script replacing a docstring's copy of a line before the code's.
- **ruff reads `result.D`, `result.E`, `result.U` as constants** (upper-case attribute names) and flags `result.D == pinned(...)` as a Yoda comparison. The pins were flipped by `ruff --fix`.
- **BSD `split` has no `-n l/5`.** The intended chunked pytest run therefore expanded to no file names and ran the whole suite: 3506 passed in 3 minutes 12 seconds, which made chunking unnecessary.
- **The host's load swung from 8 to 55 (one minute) during the work.** A first timing of v0 (0.44 seconds for six views at the worst of it) suggested the lobe fit would take minutes; the whole report, 244 grid evaluations included, took about 5 seconds, and the fixture 30 seconds, not the 5 minutes the brief expected.
- **The fix round misread the reviewer's "0.57 or lower at 0.1" as "halved at 0.1".** The first test of the reading sweep asserted that every other reading halves the excess at 0.1; under altitude 0 the ratio is 0.573 and it does not. The sweep showed it and the test was corrected to say so (three of the four halve, altitude 0 does not); the log and the report say the same.
- **Surprising: the sign of D.** Away from the sun v1's ratio to Prague falls between 0.03 and 0.1 at sun 0 (D -0.0337) after rising at sun +10 (+0.0326), the opposite of what a 6.6 percent excess in v1's multiple scattering would do. See the exploratory section.
- **Surprising: the whole-sky offset at sun 0** and the load dependence of the best asymmetry (above).

## Known limits

- **Rule A has no power at sun 0.** E is 0.0445 over P and U 0.0269; the answer waits for a reference with v1's own aerosol.
- **Rule B's verdict is defined on Prague's primary reading and has no term for the reading** (rule A's m is the analogue it lacks). Under the four other readings the ratio r(g*) / r(0.7) at 0.03 is 0.501 to 0.964 against the 0.5 bar, so "aerosol lobe" does not survive a change of reading there. A gap in the criterion, recorded, not fixed after the fact.
- **Rule B rules on six views on one azimuth.** "Aerosol lobe" is a statement about the sunward views on the solar vertical. The sky-wide offset above is neither explained nor ruled out as the larger problem.
- **XYZ_g holds the multiple scattering at asymmetry 0.7**, as the criterion specifies. A renderer with a different asymmetry would rebuild its table, so "what a renderer with g* would do" is approximate, and the control recomputation is a bound on a neighbour of v1, not on a renderer anyone has.
- **g* is an effective Henyey-Greenstein value, not a measurement of Prague's aerosol.** Prague's phase functions are per particle type and wavelength; the fit absorbs every other difference between the two models' toward-sun light (aerosol profile, extinction, ozone) into g.
- **Prague's aerosol is not v1's.** At the Koschmieder visibilities it has aerosol depths of 0.041 and 0.114 where v1 has 0.03 and 0.1 (#34), and at 0.03 no visibility in its range matches (131.8 km, clamped); its ozone is a U.S. Standard 345 DU, v1's 280.
- **Sun 0 is a node of the dataset's sun axis** (nodes at -0.5, 0, 1.0), so the sun interpolation is exact there; sun +10 lies between the nodes 8.05 and 11.25. The two suns are not equally exact and nothing here corrects for that.
- **One albedo (0.2), one ozone, two aerosol loads.** The sun +10 fit and the sun 0 ruling share them.
- **The datasets carry no licence statement of their own that I could find;** nothing of them is committed. `tests/fixtures/prague/PROVENANCE.md` describes both fixtures, and each fixture records its own provenance in the file.
