# Day 0, later: public cameras replace the home camera

**Decided:** the primary truth source is Arizona DEQ's PhoenixVis White Tank camera (`WHTM1`, looking west), with the Estrella camera (`ESMO1`, southwest) as a second view. The owner suggested trying free camera feeds; a search of agency, research-network and aggregator cameras verified each candidate by fetching it and reading its terms.

**Rejected alternatives:** a calibrated camera at the owner's home first (now optional tier B); City of Phoenix's rooftop camera (best geometry, but it is a Nest public share whose terms forbid this use); WeatherBug, Windy and ALERTWest feeds (terms forbid storage or scraping); a Tempe webcam with a 9-day archive (terms unclear, sky strip thin); PhenoCam (clean licence, but about 100 miles away with little sky).

**What it changes:** no spend, no mounting, no summer-heat risk, no home location to protect, and observations start tonight. The cost is calibration: webcams set exposure and white balance automatically, so absolute colour cannot be scored. lastlight scores colour contrasts within each frame instead, which those automatic settings cancel to first order. ADEQ's terms are silent on automated use, so frames stay private and only derived numbers are published until ADEQ agrees in writing.

**What broke:** the camera site claims a new frame every 5 minutes; the measured cadence is 15. There is no archive, so the idea of backtesting last year's monsoon is dead; collection starts now.

**Next:** collector, camera geometry calibration and the contrast-profile observation as M2 slices; permission emails drafted for the owner to send.
