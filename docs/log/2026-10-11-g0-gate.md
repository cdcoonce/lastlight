# G0: the spread study on a year of archived inputs

**Result:** PASS against the pre-registered criterion, **in renderer-v0 units**. On 323 of 365 dates (88.5 percent) the rendered within-frame contrast differs from the period median by D(d, s) of at least 0.05 in at least 2 of the 3 counted bands; the gate needs 25 percent. At the ends of the fit's pixel-scale range it is 77.0 and 88.8 percent. With the median taken over the dates within 15 days (a seasonal control, reported and not the criterion) it is 48.5 percent (log(B/G) alone 62.5 percent, 228 of 365; log(R/G) alone 12.3 percent, 45 of 365), which is what a forecast would have to beat, in renderer units. **Read this PASS in renderer-v0 units.** D(d, s) is computed on the single-scattering renderer's contrast, not on camera pixels, and the PASS rests on one channel: log(B/G) alone meets the rule on 94.2 percent of dates and log(R/G) alone on 24.9 percent; the seasonal control rests on the same channel. At a sun elevation of 0 the rendered lowest-band log(B/G) contrast is -2.62, where the four usable frames of the one camera evening so far (overcast) read between -0.21 and +0.14 in either channel, so 0.05 in these units is not 0.05 on a webcam. All aerosol variation comes from GEFS-Aerosols' modelled optical depth (a model at one 25 km cell; whether its hour-0 state assimilates aerosol observations was not verified); HRRR's smoke field was exactly zero in the analyses. This PASS says the renderer's output moves from day to day by more than 0.05 in its own units. It does not show that a camera can see that movement (G1, issue #31, measures that), and it does not yet support a claim that the forecast can beat climatology or persistence; a multiple-scattering renderer (issue #32) re-runs this gate. The report is `docs/gates/G0/report.md` (the same caveat sits directly under its verdict line), the per-date file `docs/gates/G0/results.csv`, the criterion `docs/gates/G0/criterion.md` (committed first). Issue #10.

**Decided** (the conductor's comment of 2026-10-11 replaced the criterion in the issue body; each choice below has its rejected alternative):

- **Inputs are analyses.** HRRR at forecast hour 0 for the whole UTC hour nearest the instant the sun reaches the horizon (the past-evening path of `plan_run`, unchanged), and GEFS-Aerosols at forecast hour 0 of the latest six-hourly cycle at or before the same instant. Rejected: the forecast the daily seal would have used, which mixes forecast error into a question about the real atmosphere.
- **Aerosol: NOAA GEFS-Aerosols, not NASA GEOS-FP.** The NCCS OPeNDAP server is a GrADS data server under `/dods/GEOS-5/fp/0.25_deg/assim/`; its aerosol collection is `tavg3_2d_aer_Nx` (three-hourly; the `tavg1_2d_aer_Nx` named in the plan is not there) and its time axis starts on 27 February 2026, about seven and a half of the twelve months. The file-based GEOS-FP archive would need whole global files per date. GEFS-Aerosols (`noaa-gefs-pds`, `gefs.YYYYMMDD/CC/chem/pgrb2ap25/`, the control run's 2-D product) covers the whole year, publishes an `.idx`, and carries `AOTK` at 550 nm for total aerosol and for dust, so the existing byte-range machinery applies. Rejected: GEOS-FP for the months it covers and nothing before (two methods in one study), and HRRR smoke plus the 0.06 background (no dust).
- **The archive's total replaces HRRR smoke plus background.** GEFS's total already carries smoke, so adding HRRR's would count it twice. `reduce_transect` takes an optional `AerosolColumn(total, dust, source)`; without it the reduction is byte for byte what the daily forecast has always produced (pinned in `tests/test_atmosphere_aerosol.py` against text and numbers copied from before the argument existed). Rejected: forking the reduction, and adding the dust to HRRR's total.
- **Rendering toward WHTM1's view.** `render_row` takes the view's azimuth relative to the sun (default 0, unchanged); `sky_xyz` gives the XYZ at any view elevations; `scattering_cosine` is the one place the scattering angle is computed. The relative azimuth is the fit's centre azimuth minus the sun's azimuth at each sweep elevation of each date, internal only. It runs from about 25 to 34 degrees in October and 39 to 49 in January, and about -16 to -5 in June, when the sun sets next to the frame. Rejected: the solar vertical, which has the camera looking at the sun's own azimuth, 15 degrees from where its fit says it looks.
- **Rows to elevation.** A band's elevation is the skyline's median elevation over the sky columns (here 1.11 degrees) plus the band's centre row over the nominal pixel scale (44.2 px per degree), so the five bands sit at 1.5 to 3.3 degrees and the reference at 4.0. The sensitivity runs use each end of `uncertainty.px_per_deg` (27.5 and 44.9) with the skyline kept where it is. A test paints a frame with the rendered sky and reads it with the real `contrast_profile`: the two agree to 1e-3, so the rendered quantity is the one `observe` measures.
- **Linear sRGB is the webcam proxy, with a channel floor.** Single scattering near the horizon is outside the sRGB gamut (the blue channel goes negative), and a logarithm needs a positive number, so a channel is raised to the widest ratio an 8-bit frame can show under `observe`'s own rules (code 6 against code 249, linearised, 0.0019 of the pixel's largest channel). The floor is derived from `observe`'s constants in code and used for 2.2 percent of the rendered channels. Rejected: dropping the pixel (as `observe` would) because it would leave D undefined wherever a channel is out of gamut, and clipping at zero because the logarithm is undefined there.
- **Pre-registration.** The first commit adds `criterion.md` with the criterion verbatim (a test finds the sentence in the file and in the code) and the definitions the sentence leaves open: sweep elevations in a band are `lower < e <= upper`, with `late` also holding -8 (12, 4, 6 and 7 of the 29), the median is per elevation, band and channel, "25 percent of dates" is of the dates used, the sky columns are those where every band and the reference lie in the sky mask, and the five most extreme dates are ranked by mean D over the counted bands. A later commit adds an amendments section for the three post hoc additions below; the criterion and the verdict do not change.
- **Resumable and polite.** `lastlight g0 --stage fetch` collects each date that has no record in `~/.cache/lastlight/g0/records` and writes it at once; a rerun skips it without a request. `--stage report` reads only the cache. Requests go through `PoliteTransport` (at most two a second, never parallel, retries with a growing pause, a 404 never retried); a date that is not in an archive is a record with a reason, a network fault is raised and the date is tried again next run. Rejected: reusing the byte-range cache alone for the offline pass, because the `.idx` is always re-read.
- **Tier B.** Delta-E 2000 of the forecast's own absolute curve (solar vertical, seven view elevations) against the period's median curve, by band `early`, `mid` and `late`, with no criterion: medians 3.1, 2.8 and 3.0, maxima above 31.

**Findings** (the data, and what they mean for reading the verdict):

- **HRRR's smoke field is zero in the analysis.** `AOTK` at forecast hour 0 was exactly zero at every point of every transect on all 365 dates. A check of the whole grid on two dates (2025-09-10, 2026-08-20) found it zero everywhere at hour 0 and non-zero at hours 6 and 12. So with analyses HRRR carries no aerosol information, and all of the aerosol variation here is GEFS's. The daily forecast reads forecast hours, which do carry smoke.
- **The verdict is carried by log(B/G).** Split by channel (post hoc, labelled in the report): log(B/G) alone is met on 94.2 percent of dates, log(R/G) alone on 24.9 percent (91 of 365, one date short of 25). log(B/G) carries 87 percent of the summed D over the counted bands. In linear sRGB the blue channel of these reddened low skies is nearly zero, and a log ratio against it is very sensitive; the median rendered log(B/G) contrast of the lowest band at a sun elevation of 0 is -2.6, where the four frames of the first observed evening (overcast) read -0.2 to +0.1. Single scattering leaves out the multiple scattering that fills the blue of a twilight sky.
- **One real dust event.** 2026-03-28 to 2026-03-30: GEFS total optical depth 0.21, 0.47 and 0.34 (dust 0.15, 0.41 and 0.27), D(d, s) of 1.0 to 2.5 in the early and mid bands on the 29th and 30th. Aerosol is low in autumn and winter (median 0.015 to 0.025), 0.05 to 0.07 from April to August.
- **The season matters but does not decide it.** The year-wide median counts the sun's azimuth and the ozone column as variation; the 15-day control gives 48.5 percent, still above 25. It rests on the same channel: log(B/G) alone 62.5 percent (228 of 365), log(R/G) alone 12.3 percent (45 of 365, below 25).

**What broke:**

- The privacy scanner (P003) flagged a test that named the GEFS cell's latitude and longitude on one line. The cell is now described by its grid row and column.
- GEFS's `.idx` ends its last message without an upper bound, so the dust message (the last of the two selected, and the last of the cropped fixture) has an open-ended range, as in the HRRR code; the first version of the fixture support gave it a closed one and two tests failed.
- `sort_keys=True` in the record writer reordered the pixel scales, and the record check, which compared the keys as an ordered tuple, refused its own file; it compares sets now and restores the order on load.
- The unedited GEFS fixture is the right run for the evening before its date, so the test for "a message from another run" first passed by accident; it serves a re-dated crop instead.
- The first figures set the axis from the maximum, so one dust storm flattened 364 dates into a bar; the axes now stop at a high percentile and say how many dates are past them.

**Review fixes (PR #30).** An adversarial review mutated the code 134 times (113 killed, 21 survived) and found the arithmetic, the pre-registration and the data sound. The verdict, the criterion, the rule constants and every input are unchanged: the regenerated `results.csv` and figures are byte for byte the committed ones (`cmp`), and the verdict still reads PASS, 323 of 365. Three things were blocking.

- **The verdict section did not say what the PASS is made of.** It now carries, directly under the verdict line and generated from the study, that the PASS is in renderer-v0 units and rests on log(B/G) (94.2 percent of dates against 24.9 for log(R/G); the seasonal control the same, 62.5 against 12.3), that the rendered contrast at a sun elevation of 0 is -2.62 where the one observed evening reads between -0.21 and +0.14, that all aerosol variation is GEFS's, and that the PASS neither shows a camera can see the movement nor supports a claim that the forecast beats climatology or persistence. A FAIL gets the adapted paragraph. Claims that depend on the data (one channel carries it; the seasonal control rests on the same one; HRRR's smoke was zero) are made only when the data say so.
- **A D of exactly 0.05 was untested.** A built distance matrix now holds the `>=`.
- **Five GEFS message guards could not fail a test** (parameter, level, aerosol type, forecast hour, missing wavelength band). Each is now refused by an edited message and matched on its own text. The old dust-and-total swap test had never reached the aerosol-type guard: the range check refused it first, because the dust message is shorter than the total's range. Its idx now carries the swapped file's offsets.

Also taken: the sensitivity's median computed from the shifted contrast, the drop-detail sanitiser (URL, file name, length cap, whitespace), the seasonal window edge, the time-budget edge, the figure axis percentile, and the GEFS guards no test could fail (idx label, level, equal offsets, a short range, packing noise, a second grid). `is_not_found` looked for 404 in the message text; it now reads the status field after the URL, so a 5xx for a run of 4 April (404 is in the URL) is retried and not recorded as a missing file. The report credits NOAA (derived values, no endorsement implied), says the aerosol input is a modelled optical depth at one 25 km cell and that whether its hour-0 state assimilates aerosol observations was not verified, and gives the seasonal control by channel. The reviewer's mutation specs are committed under `tests/teeth/g0-*.json` and run from the repository root with `teeth_check.py`: 174 rows, 165 killed, the declared control held in each of the nine files that has one, none survived (152 distinct mutants; two files re-run earlier rows against wider test sets).
