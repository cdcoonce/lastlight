# Renderer v1: the camera magnitude check and the G0 re-run (#35)

**Decided:** `lastlight renderer-check` implements `docs/gates/renderer-v1/criterion.md` and nothing else, and `lastlight g0 --engine v1` re-runs G0 with renderer v1 into a sibling store. The criterion, its amendment and G0's criterion were not touched; no number below was tuned after it was seen.

- **The check** (`lastlight.renderer_check`, command `renderer-check --observed-dir DIR --out DIR [--cache-dir DIR] [--stage fetch|report|all]`) reads WHTM1's private `observed.json` files, collects each considered evening's inputs once into `<cache-dir>/renderer-v1/`, renders v0 and v1 for every used frame through G0's seam (`sky_xyz`, then `log_chromaticity` with its channel floor), and writes `report.md`, `evenings.csv` and `frames.csv`: dates, sun elevations (two decimals) and derived numbers only. Exit 0 done, 1 done but an evening has no input record yet, 2 usage or input error, 3 refusal (nothing is written to `--out`).
- **Evenings and frames.** An evening is considered from 2026-10-10 on with at least 3 used frames; a frame is used when `bands[0].log_bg` is not null, and nothing else excludes one (the light-dome flag, the other bands and the pixel counts are not read). Every field of an observed file is checked before use (a bool is not a number, non-finite floats and numbers too large for a float are refused, bytes decode with replacement), and a file with another camera, another schema version or a `date` that disagrees with its name is refused with one line naming the file and the field.
- **Inputs per evening.** `aod_550` and `ozone_du` come from G0's `Collector` and its `AtmosphereRecord` unchanged, so an evening G0 would drop is dropped with G0's reason. The cloud is HRRR `TCDC` (entire atmosphere) at forecast hour 0 of the target-hour cycle, fetched with `hrrr.fetch_fields` into the shared byte cache and averaged over G0's 17 transect points; the evening counts when the mean is at most 10 percent (exactly 10 counts, summed exactly). Until the target-hour cycle is two hours old the evening gets no record and is retried on the next run: a forecast is never substituted.
- **Rendering.** The sun elevation is the frame's recorded one, never snapped; the relative azimuth is the fit's centre azimuth minus the sun's azimuth at that elevation, found by bisection on `geometry.solar_position` (only rendered numbers leave the module). The verdict uses WHTM1's nominal view; the two ends of the pixel-scale range are reported only. r_e is a ratio of two medians of absolute values and is `inf` for a zero observed median, which still takes part in the median. The verdict is the median of the v1 r_e of the 5 earliest counted evenings, bounds included.
- **Refusals** (before any verdict, and before any fetch in the `all` stage): the SHA-256 of the bytes of the table file the renderer loads against a constant in the check's own module (also written in the criterion, and pinned by a test), and three probes of `sky_xyz(engine="v1")` against the values registered with v1 (relative 1e-6, absolute floor 1e-12). The same probes for v0 are pinned in a test of the check's v0 path, as a regression pin and not a refusal.
- **G0 `--engine v1`** re-renders each date that has an ok v0 record from its `AtmosphereRecord` (`aod_total`, `ozone_du`, every other `Atmosphere` field at its default, as G0 builds it) with `day_contrasts(..., engine="v1")` per scale and `render_curve(..., engine="v1")`, into `<cache-dir>/g0-v1/records/`, resumably and with no network; a dropped v0 record stays dropped with the same reason; the report stage reads only that store and writes the v1 report, which names renderer v1 where the v0 report names v0. The v0 files in `docs/gates/G0/` are byte for byte unchanged (`git diff --stat origin/main` over them is empty, and `cmp` of each against `origin/main` agrees), and the v0 report regenerated from the real cache with the new code is byte for byte the committed one, figures included.

**Rejected alternatives:**

- Adding `TCDC` to G0's `HRRR_FIELD_NAMES` so one download serves both: it would change what every G0 run downloads and reads, and the spec forbids a change to G0's behaviour. The cost is one extra byte-range request per evening.
- Asking `hrrr.plan_run` for its default plan and checking the forecast hour: when the cycle is not two hours old that path calls `latest_run` on the network just to be told "not ready". The check asks for forecast hour 0 (no request) and compares the cycle's age with its own two-hour constant; a test pins agreement with `plan_run`'s default decision at five clocks around the boundary.
- Snapping each frame's elevation to G0's half-degree sweep and reusing `sun_azimuths`: it is exactly what definition 6 rules out (the lowest band's v0 contrast moves by 18 percent over 0.13 degrees at sun 1.4). Calling `geometry._crossing_epoch` for the azimuth was rejected as a private import; the 20 lines of bisection are tested against the sweep's azimuths to 0.02 degrees.
- Comparing the table's digest with `lastlight.render.v1.TABLE_SHA256`: a change to the table and to that pin could be made together. A test sets that pin to the changed table's digest and the check still refuses.
- Putting an `engine` argument through every function of the G0 report: the study carries its engine (`Study.engine`, default `v0`) and the report reads it, so no existing signature changed and the v0 text is the same code path.
- A new drop reason name for the check's table failure: the one reason `v1_outside_table` serves both the G0 re-run and the check.

**Additions the spec did not state (interpretations, each pinned by a test):**

- A record dropped for `hrrr_run_missing` or `aerosol_run_missing` is fetched again while the evening is less than two calendar days old. GEFS-Aerosols' cycle at the sunset is published hours after the evening, so a 404 within hours of it is a publication lag and not a fact about the archive; recording it as final would take the evening out of the first 5 for good. Other reasons are final, and so is a missing run after two days.
- A counted evening dated after the earliest evening still waiting for its inputs holds no first-5 slot (the report says "waiting for an earlier evening's inputs"): the earlier one may still turn out to be counted and then it comes first, and the criterion computes the verdict once.
- The fetch stage alone does not run the refusals: it produces cache, not a verdict.
- A pixel-scale end that v1's table does not cover gives `n/a` for that end instead of dropping the evening; and a null `log_rg` leaves that frame out of the log(R/G) ratio only ("the same frames").

**What broke:**

- **The independent route to a rendered value was less precise than the code under test.** The tests render sweep-elevation frames through G0's `sun_azimuths`, whose bisection stops within one second (about 0.003 degrees of azimuth); the check's own search stops within a millisecond. The first run differed in the sixth digit (-0.534882 against -0.534884) and a relative 1e-9 test failed. The tolerance is now 1e-4, still a hundred times finer than the difference between a snapped and an unsnapped elevation.
- **The recorded HRRR crop's total cloud cover is not 100 everywhere.** A test expected exactly 100 percent; the 17 transect points average 99.65, which also shows the mean is taken over the transect and not read at one cell.
- **The test rig defaulted to the `all` stage**, so an evening with no record tried to fetch it through a transport that fails the test on any request. Report-stage tests now say so.
- **The privacy scanner flagged my own test**: a made-up latitude and longitude pair that happened to fall inside Arizona. The test points are now elsewhere; the scanner and its allowlist are unchanged.
- The host ran at a load average of several hundred; nothing timed out, but each test file took a minute or more.

**Real outputs.**

- Registered table digest and the v1 fingerprint matched on this checkout.
- `lastlight renderer-check` over WHTM1's three observed evenings: **NOT YET DECIDABLE (0 of 5 counted evenings)**. All three evenings were considered (4 used frames each) and none was clear enough to count: 2026-10-10 cloud 99.12 percent, 2026-10-11 cloud 31.41 percent, 2026-10-12 cloud 49.53 percent. Their reported-only r_e for v1 are 5.91, 0.69 and 0.25, and for v0 25.41, 10.72 and 4.91; they enter no verdict.
- G0 re-run with v1 over 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30 (365 dates, none dropped): **PASS, 122 of 365 dates meet the rule (33.4 percent; 25 percent needed)**, beside v0's **PASS, 323 of 365 (88.5 percent)**. The pass rests on log(B/G) alone in both (v1: B/G alone 59.5 percent of dates and R/G alone 8.8 percent; v0: 94.2 and 24.9). The seasonal control gives 115 of 365 (31.5 percent) for v1 against 177 (48.5 percent) for v0. At a sun elevation of 0 the median lowest-band log(B/G) contrast is -0.38 with v1 and -2.62 with v0. The pixel-scale sensitivity is reported, not part of the verdict, and it matters: at the low end of the range v1 would be 82 of 365 (22.5 percent), a FAIL, at the nominal scale a PASS, and at the high end 126 (34.5 percent).
- The v1 re-render of the year took about ten minutes, in two chunks under `--budget-seconds`.

**Known limits:** three evenings is all there is, and none is clear; the verdict needs five clear ones, so it will wait on the weather. The check renders v0 as well as v1 for every frame, which is cheap here (four frames an evening). The cloud filter uses the model's analysis, not a measurement.

**Next:** keep running `lastlight observe` on clear evenings and `lastlight renderer-check` after each; adoption (switching `seal-daily` to v1 and bumping the package version) is a separate, later change that this verdict, once it exists, gates.
