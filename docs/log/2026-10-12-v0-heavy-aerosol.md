# Renderer v0 under heavy aerosol: an adaptive quadrature, and the registered one left alone

**Decided:** v0's single scattering gets a `quadrature` keyword, `"registered"` (the default, the integration on `main` at 807ed34, bit for bit) or `"adaptive"`, on `sky_spectrum`, `sky_xyz`, `render_row` and `render_curve` for both engines (issue #40). `lastlight.render.QUADRATURES` lists them; anything else is a `ValueError` naming the two. Only the daily forecast's v0 painting (`lastlight.painting.paint`, which `seal-daily` calls) asks for `"adaptive"`; renderer v1's painting, G0 and the renderer-v1 camera check keep the registered integration by not passing the keyword. The registered integration is renderer v1's inherited single scattering, and v1 is pre-registered (`docs/gates/renderer-v1/criterion.md`, definition 8: "a later change to v1's physics or table is a different renderer"), so changing the default would have changed the renderer the magnitude check pins.

**Not done:** the package version is still 0.1.0, not 0.1.1 (see What broke). Until it is bumped, the ledger index's `model_version` does not separate paintings made before and after this change. Paintings at ordinary aerosol loads are byte-identical either way; only heavy-aerosol evenings differ.

## The cause

The issue guessed the 50 m shells near the ground against a long, low, extinguishing sightline. The sightline is the culprit; the shells are not.

`sky_spectrum` cuts the sightline at the shell boundaries and evaluates each piece once, in its middle: the sunlight arriving there, the share scattered toward the observer, and the transmittance back to the observer. For a piece of optical depth D along the view, the true integral of the falling transmittance is (1 - e^-D) / D of the piece's length, and the midpoint sample is e^(-D/2). The sample is below the integral by 4.1 percent at D = 1, 14.9 percent at D = 2 and 44.9 percent at D = 4.

How thick the pieces are, as the largest optical depth of any piece at any wavelength, view 1.5066 degrees (WHTM1's lowest band):

| `aod_550` | 0.03 | 0.06 | 0.1 | 0.3 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| largest piece depth, any wavelength | 0.56 | 0.60 | 0.75 | 1.54 | 2.33 | 4.30 |
| same, at 550 nm | 0.22 | 0.31 | 0.43 | 1.01 | 1.59 | 3.05 |

From `aod_550` 0.1 up the thickest piece is the first 250 m shell above 1 km (7.7 km long at that view), not one of the 50 m shells (1.9 km long). Evidence, from the sweep of the issue against `_reference_radiance` (all with the cached brute-force sums of the grid below):

1. **The error follows the piece depth.** At `aod_550` 1.0, sun +6, 550 nm, azimuth 0, registered over brute force is 0.911 at a 1.0 degree view, 0.814 at 0.7, 0.390 at 0.3 and 0.005 at 0 (the lowest 50 m shell is then 25 km long). It falls with the view, and so with the piece length, and it is largest where the sun lights the near ground (sun +6 and +3) and nearly absent at twilight, where the near pieces are in shadow.
2. **Cutting the same pieces finer removes it, with the same shells.** Splitting every shell's piece of the sightline into 2, 4, 8, 16 or 32 equal parts, each part keeping its shell's densities (so the model is unchanged and only the sampling is finer), moves the worst error over the grid from -5.78 percent to +1.81 percent at `aod_550` 1.0 (stable from a split of 2 on), and from -8.87 percent to -0.74 percent for the thin layer (scale height 0.6 km, `aod_550` 0.6). The sun +6, azimuth 30, 1.5 degree, 450 nm point of the issue goes from -5.78 to -0.13 percent.
3. **The brute-force sum is not the problem.** Raising its resolution from 4000 view steps and 600 sun steps to 32000 and 4800 moves it by -0.19 percent at `aod_550` 1.0, -0.71 percent in the thin layer (the committed reference is that much high there) and 0.00 percent at `aod_550` 0.5, sun -3. The 2 percent bound is meaningful.
4. **The remaining error is another effect.** After refinement the worst error at twilight (sun -3 and 0) is 1.2 to 1.8 percent *high*, at every refinement level. That is the shells' mean densities along the sunward path, which grazes a shell at low sun, not the integral along the view; it is in the registered result too, where the under-reading of item 1 partly cancels it.

## The fix

`transfer._sightline_cuts` decides where the sightline is cut. `"registered"` cuts at the shell boundaries, exactly as before. `"adaptive"` finds the largest optical depth of any piece at any wavelength; if it is at most `ADAPTIVE_TRIGGER_DEPTH` (0.7) it returns the registered cuts, so the result is the registered one, bit for bit; otherwise every shell's piece is cut into equal parts (equal in height, so in optical depth, the shell being uniform) of at most `ADAPTIVE_PIECE_DEPTH` (0.1), at most `ADAPTIVE_MAX_PARTS` (128) parts per shell. The parts keep their shell's scattering and extinction, and the sunward path still goes through the model's shells. Nothing else in `sky_spectrum` changed: the same arithmetic runs on a longer list of pieces. `sky_xyz`, `render_row` and `render_curve` pass the keyword through for both engines (v1's single-scattering part uses it; the table's multiple scattering does not depend on it).

The constants: the trigger is just above the thickest piece at the ordinary loads (0.60 at `aod_550` 0.06 and 1.5 degrees; the painting's own views are higher and thinner), so the clean and ordinary skies are the registered ones; a piece under 0.1 has a midpoint error of 0.05 percent. The cap only matters for views near the horizon or absurd loads; `aod_550` 1.0 at 1.5 degrees needs at most 44 parts per shell and 692 pieces in all, against 131.

## Accuracy

Worst relative error against `_reference_radiance` over the whole grid of the issue: `aod_550` 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0, and the thin layer; sun +6, +3, 0, -3, -6; relative azimuth 0 and 30; WHTM1's five band elevations and its reference elevation (1.5066 to 3.9944 degrees); 450, 550 and 650 nm. That is 1080 brute-force sums, run once offline with a throwaway script; the test suite runs a subset (below).

| `aod_550` | registered | adaptive | where the adaptive worst is (sun, azimuth, view, nm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.03 | +0.64 percent | +0.64 percent (identical) | -3, 0, 2.41, 650 |
| 0.1 | +0.85 percent | +1.24 percent | -3, 0, 1.51, 650 |
| 0.3 | +1.47 percent | +1.73 percent | -3, 0, 1.51, 650 |
| 0.5 | +1.71 percent | +1.81 percent | -3, 0, 1.51, 650 |
| 1.0 | -5.78 percent | +1.81 percent | 0, 0, 1.51, 450 |
| thin layer (0.6 km, 0.6) | -8.87 percent | +0.79 percent | -3, 30, 1.51, 450 |

The numbers in the issue's table (0.80, 0.80, 1.38, 1.61, 5.84, 11) came from PR #39's own sweep; the grid here gives slightly different registered values. The 11 percent of the thin layer is at sun +10 and exactly 1.5 degrees, outside this grid: registered is 11.3, 9.5 and 8.3 percent low at 450, 550 and 650 nm there, adaptive 0.6, 0.6 and 0.6 percent. An independent random draw of 70 geometries off the grid (aod 0.05 to 0.9, sun +10 to -7, views 1.5 to 6, azimuth 0 to 90) found adaptive within 1.3 percent everywhere except one point that registered shares exactly (2.5 percent, below).

**Adaptive is not uniformly closer to the brute-force sum.** At `aod_550` 0.1 to 0.5 its worst error is a little *larger* than registered's (+1.24 against +0.85, +1.73 against +1.47, +1.81 against +1.71 percent), because the registered under-reading was cancelling part of the shells' positive twilight bias (item 4 above). All of it is under the 2 percent bound the issue asks for, with 0.19 points to spare at `aod_550` 0.5 and 1.0.

**Where adaptive equals registered.** Exactly (`numpy.array_equal`) at `aod_550` 0, 0.03 and 0.06 at seven views from 1.5066 to 90 degrees, four suns (+6, 0, -3, -8) and three azimuths, and for the whole `render_curve` at 0.06. Between 0.07 and 0.1 the two differ by at most 0.49 percent (`aod_550` 0.1, 1.5066 degrees, sun -3, azimuth 0).

**The trigger is a step, and the sky jumps across it.** An atmosphere just under the trigger is the registered result and one just over it is refined, so the two differ by the registered quadrature's own error at that load, which adaptive removes on the refined side only. It is the lit edge of Earth's shadow falling inside one piece (the first Known limit below), so it is at twilight: sun -1.0 to -2.0. Measured for the default aerosol shape by bisecting the load at which the trigger fires for each view and comparing the two sides (sun +6, +3, 0, -1, -1.5, -2, -3 and -6): luminance Y moves by up to 4.40 percent for a 10 degree view (`aod_550` 0.868, sun -1.0), 2.14 percent at 3 degrees (0.215, sun -1.5), 1.10, 0.76 and 1.10 percent at 1.5, 2 and 5 degrees. The painting's rows (`render_row`, sun +6 to -8 in half degrees, azimuth 0) move by up to 1.59 dE76 at the 10 degree view (sun -1.0), 0.46 at 5 degrees and 0.26 at 2 degrees; the 1.5 and 3 degree views are not painted. A single spectral entry can move more than the luminance (up to 6.3 percent at 10 degrees, sun -1.0, azimuth 0, in a scan of sun +6 to -8 in half degrees and azimuths 0, 30 and 90 over every wavelength). `test_the_jump_across_the_adaptive_trigger_stays_under_five_percent_in_luminance` holds the luminance figure to 5 percent at 2, 3, 5 and 10 degrees; it bounds the documented jump and does not detect a different trigger by itself (a trigger of 1.5 or 2.0 breaks it at 10 degrees, 3.5 does not).

**What the tests run.** `tests/test_render_physics.py` runs 30 brute-force sums per aerosol load (all five suns, both azimuths and all three wavelengths at the lowest band; the middle and reference bands at the two suns that separate the effects, +6 and -3) and 21 for the thin layer (seven sun and azimuth pairs, at 1.5 degrees, three wavelengths), plus two views below the camera's (0.7 and 1.0 degrees) and a positive control that registered fails the same 2 percent bound at the known-bad points. About 175 sums, 0.08 s of CPU each, against the 1080 of the full grid. `tests/test_render_quadrature.py` pins the registered values from `main` at 807ed34 (six cases, both engines, default and explicit keyword, tolerance 1e-9), the keyword's contract on every entry point, the exact identity at low loads, and that `paint` asks for `"adaptive"` with v0 and `"registered"` with v1 (a spy on the painting seam, once through `seal_daily`, with no network).

Every brute-force comparison is graded at 2 percent relative with no absolute floor (`pytest.approx(reference, rel=0.02, abs=0.0)`). pytest's default floor of 1e-12 had graded the 29 of 173 swept points whose radiance is below 5e-11 (down to 7e-22 at `aod_550` 1.0, the dim twilight sky) far looser than 2 percent, so a per-piece depth of 0.5 instead of 0.1, which reads 3.94 percent high at one of them, passed. The review round added tests for what the first sweep could not see: the grazing views 0.1 and 0.3 degrees (the 128-part cap), the cap itself at `aod_550` 1000, the trigger reading the thickest wavelength, the refined cuts being equal parts from each shell's floor, renderer v1 forwarding the keyword through `render_row` and `render_curve`, G0's contrast and collector staying on the registered quadrature (a spy on `sky_xyz` and `render_curve` at `aod_550` 1.0), and the jump across the trigger staying under 5 percent in luminance. They add about 2 seconds to the suite (the jump test 1.0 s over its four views, most of it the first call; the G0 test 0.5 s; the v1 forwarding test 0.3 s; the rest under 0.1 s each).

## Speed

`render_curve(Atmosphere(aod_550=1.0, ozone_du=300.0), quadrature="adaptive")`: 0.20 s of CPU and 0.59 s of wall time, against 0.12 s and 0.42 s registered, at a load average near 500 on a 10-core machine (one warm call each). The limit is 10 s. `tests/test_render_quadrature.py` asserts it.

## G0

G0's report does not move: `lastlight.g0` calls `render_curve` and `sky_xyz` without the keyword, so it stays on the registered integration and nothing under `src/lastlight/g0` or the renderer-v1 check was edited. What adaptive would change over the G0 year was measured with a throwaway script outside the repository (not committed; it reads `~/.cache/lastlight/g0/records` and writes nothing). For each of the 365 cached records (all ok) it builds `Atmosphere(aod_550=aod_total, ozone_du=ozone_du)`, takes the camera's azimuth minus the sun's at sun elevation 0 (`g0.view.sun_azimuths`, `relative_azimuth`), renders WHTM1's nominal view with each quadrature (`sky_xyz`, then G0's `log_chromaticity`) and takes the lowest band's log(B/G) minus the reference band's. Command: `uv run python -I g0_year.py`. Its registered values equal the cached ones to 1e-9 on all 365 records, so the script reproduces G0's contrast.

- `aod_total` over the year: 0.007 to 0.471, median 0.034.
- Adaptive changes the contrast on 32 of the 365 dates, all with `aod_total` 0.089 or more; the other 333 are identical.
- Largest absolute change: 0.0014, on 2026-03-30 (`aod_total` 0.342; the contrast goes from 2.4614 to 2.4628). Next: 0.0005 on 2026-03-29 (0.471), 0.0004 on 2025-12-31 (0.160).
- Largest relative change: 0.126 percent, on 2026-03-28 (`aod_total` 0.208; -0.1308 to -0.1307, a contrast near zero).
- Median absolute change 0; 95th percentile 0.00008. The year's contrast range (-3.93 to 4.22) does not move.

The G0 year never had heavy aerosol, so there is nothing to carry into a re-run of the report.

## Decisions and the alternatives not taken

- **A new keyword, default registered, for both engines.** Rejected: making adaptive the default for both engines (or only v0). Renderer v1's single scattering is v0's code path and v1 is pre-registered for issue #35's check, which pins v1's output at probes; the change would have been to the registered renderer, and the criterion calls that a different renderer needing a new registration. Rejected: v1's painting also asking for adaptive, for the same reason. It does not: `QUADRATURE_BY_ENGINE` in `painting.py` maps v0 to adaptive and v1 to registered.
- **Do it now.** Rejected: waiting for v1's verdict (five counted evenings away). Nothing here touches v1, and a smoke evening is exactly when the forecast matters.
- **Leave the criterion alone.** Rejected: amending `docs/gates/renderer-v1/criterion.md` (it forbids changes after a result and allows amendments only to record which v1 is under test). Nothing needed amending, because v1's registered output is unchanged.
- **Refine the sampling, not the model.** Rejected: a finer shell grid (`LEVELS_KM`). It fixes this and moves every v0 number, the registered ones included, and every test and pin built on them. Rejected: integrating each piece's transmittance analytically ((1 - e^-D) / D). It would be exact for the view but changes the arithmetic at every load, so there would be no exact identity at ordinary loads, and it leaves the sun-path sampling as it is.
- **A trigger on the largest piece depth.** Rejected: a trigger on the contribution-weighted midpoint error estimate (the sum of each piece's contribution times D squared over 24). It tracks the deviation better at some loads, and equally misses the effects that do not come from the view's optical depth (the sun-path sampling, the lit edge of the shadow); it needs the registered pass first and a tolerance of its own. The raw rule is one number the reader can check. Its price is that the trigger is also set off by a thick piece that carries little weight (the highest Rayleigh shells at 380 nm); that only means a more accurate integral than needed, and adaptive differs from registered by at most 0.49 percent up to `aod_550` 0.1.
- **Refine always.** Rejected: refining at any load moves the recorded evening's curve (`aod_550` 0.062) by up to 0.21 in L*, 0.13 in a* and 0.23 in b*, so every painting pin of that evening would have to be regenerated. With the trigger they pass unchanged.

## Known limits

- **A different error, left alone.** Registered (and unrefined adaptive) can be 2.5 percent off the brute-force sum at ordinary loads when the lit edge of Earth's shadow falls inside one piece: `aod_550` 0.2, sun -1.5, view 3.0, azimuth 15, 550 nm (outside the grid; splitting the pieces brings it to 0.7 percent). The lit test is made once per piece, so the edge is resolved to the piece. Fixing it would move ordinary paintings, which this change is built not to do. It is not a heavy-aerosol effect and not what the issue asks.
- **The trigger is a step.** The sky jumps across `ADAPTIVE_TRIGGER_DEPTH` by up to 4.4 percent in luminance and 1.6 dE76 in a painting's row (see Accuracy). It is the registered quadrature's error at that load, which adaptive removes on the refined side only; smoothing it would mean refining at every load, which moves the ordinary paintings this change is built not to move.
- **The shells' mean densities** along the sunward path leave adaptive up to 1.8 percent high at low sun and low views under aerosol (item 4 of the cause).
- **Level views.** At exactly 0 degrees (`aod_550` 1.0, sun +6, azimuth 0, 550 nm) adaptive is 6 percent below the brute-force sum (the cap of 128 parts is in force; not investigated further); at 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0 degrees it is within 0.1 percent. No camera band or painting view is below 1.5 degrees.

## What broke

- **The version bump is blocked, and I did not make it.** The spec asks for 0.1.0 to 0.1.1 in `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` so the ledger index's model version separates the two kinds of painting. The version is also written in `src/lastlight/__init__.py` (`__version__`, which `painting.paint` stamps into every forecast), and existing tests pin it: bumping `pyproject.toml` and `__init__.py` together makes 8 existing tests fail (measured, the rest of 203 in `test_cli.py`, `test_painting.py`, `test_render_v1_painting.py`, `test_forecast.py`, `test_daily.py` and `test_ledger_branch.py` pass): `test_cli.py::test_package_exposes_version`, `::test_version_flag_prints_name_and_version_and_returns_zero`, `::test_module_entry_point_prints_version_and_exits_zero`, `::test_console_script_prints_version_and_exits_zero`; `test_painting.py::test_the_model_block_names_the_v0_renderer_the_version_and_the_code`; `test_render_v1_painting.py::test_the_default_engine_paints_the_same_painting_as_before_this_slice`, `::test_the_default_model_is_named_lastlight_v0` and `::test_painting_with_v1_names_the_model_lastlight_v1_and_keeps_the_version` (the first of these also compares against `tests/fixtures/painting_v0_before_v1.json`, whose model block says 0.1.0). Bumping needs `src/lastlight/__init__.py` and edits to those tests and that fixture, none of which this change may touch. Without the bump all of them pass, which also shows the painting itself did not move at the recorded evening's aerosol load.
- **My first test draft hid its own green tests.** The new test module imported `QUADRATURES` at the top, so on unchanged code the whole file failed at collection and the registered pins could not show they were already green. It now reaches the constant through the package, and on unchanged `main` the six default-quadrature pins pass (the values were copied right) while everything that needs the keyword fails with `TypeError: ... unexpected keyword argument 'quadrature'`. A keyword-only test I wrote first also passed for the wrong reason (any extra positional argument is a `TypeError`); it now inspects the signatures.
- **Two assertions of mine were wrong, not the code.** One divided by an all-zero spectrum for a sky wholly in Earth's shadow (the geometry sweep now skips it); one asserted adaptive within 50 percent of registered for a level view, where registered is up to two orders of magnitude low (it now asserts adaptive finite and never below registered, and puts the accuracy check at 0.7 and 1.0 degrees against the brute-force sum).
- **The first idea for the trigger was too tight.** A threshold of 0.6 would have left aod 0.06 at 1.5 degrees (0.595) on the edge and made a pressure of 1040 hPa enough to refine a clean sky. 0.7 leaves margin at the ordinary loads and the 0.1 target does the accuracy.
