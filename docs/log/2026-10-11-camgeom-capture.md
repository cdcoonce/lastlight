# Camera geometry: a guard against a cloud edge capturing the least-squares stage

**Decided:** `fit_orientation` now checks stage 3 (the robust least-squares fit onto the traced skyline) against the climb it started from, and records the verdict (issue #28). After stage 3 each frame's edge map scores the climbed and the fitted skyline. When most frames score the fitted one more than a fifth lower, the least-squares result is refused, stage 3 is redone without the traced columns that lie more than 8 px from the climbed skyline, and that refit is accepted only if it does not lose in the same way; if it does, the climbed orientation is the result. The verdict is written to an optional object of schema 2, `least_squares_guard`, by every fit made from now on. `WHTM1.json` is not rewritten: it has no such object, loads as before, and its SHA-256 (cited by every observation) is pinned by a test.

**The capture, reproduced** (`tests/test_camgeom_capture.py`). The 30 degree camera of the neighbouring stage scenes, three noisy frames, and `paint_cloud_base` painting a bright cloud with a dark underside 20 rows above the ridge over the last 200 columns of every frame. The climb is exact (0.001 degrees). The code before the guard ended 0.39 degrees off in azimuth, 0.23 in elevation, 1.63 in roll and 6.3 percent in field of view, with 4.8 px of residual against the traced cloud.

Scenes tried, all on that camera, with the cloud in all three frames and its underside 40 or 60 below white:

- a cloud 15, 20 and 25 rows above the ridge, and a ramp from 15 to 25 in both directions;
- over 200 columns at either edge, the fit was captured at every offset (azimuth 0.24 to 0.48 degrees, roll 0.8 to 2.0, field of view 3.2 to 7.7 percent);
- over the first 300 columns, the climb itself was sometimes drawn to the cloud as well (a different failure, not addressed here);
- over 150 columns at the right edge, captured at 15 rows (azimuth 0.06, roll 0.7, field of view 1.7 percent) and not at 25;
- over 100 columns at either edge, not captured;
- over the middle 160 columns, not captured: the narrower bands drop it (the existing test).

At the edge the same cloud is not dropped, because the wide first pass has already moved the fit onto it and the narrow bands then centre on the moved fit.

**What the guard does, and the finding that shaped it.** The conductor's spec compared `_energy(mean_strength, ...)` of the climbed and the fitted orientation: the score the climb maximises, on the mean of the frames' edge maps. Read literally, that refuses a correct fit. `test_a_strong_cloud_edge_in_one_frame_does_not_pull_the_fit_off_a_faint_ridge` puts a dark cloud underside nine times stronger than the faint ridge in one frame. The climb on the mean map is drawn to the cloud (8 px RMS off, 0.28 degrees low), the median of the traces outvotes the cloud, and least squares puts the camera back on the ridge. On the mean map the fitted skyline then scores 4.00 and the climbed one 11.63, a loss of 65 percent, which is more than any captured scene loses. No margin separates the two cases (the captured scenes lose 33 to 44 percent), and with the literal rule at a 20 percent margin the guard keeps the climb and that test fails, 0.28 degrees low in elevation and 0.38 percent low in field of view. Frame by frame the picture is the opposite: the climb scores 34.4, 0.26 and 0.26 on the three frames and the fitted skyline 1.3, 5.4 and 5.3. The cloud frame loses, and the other two, which show the ridge, gain twenty times over.

So the vote is by frame, as the median of the traces is. A frame votes against the fitted skyline when it scores that skyline lower than the climbed one by more than the margin on its own edge map, and the result is refused when more than half of the frames vote. A cloud held in one frame is outvoted. A fit that a cloud held in most frames has captured is off the ridge, which every frame holds, so it loses in all of them (43, 44 and 43 percent for the persistent-edge scene; a cloud in two of three frames also loses 43, 44 and 57 percent). The energies in the fit file are the mean-map scores of the spec, so they compare with the coarse search's and the climb's; the decision uses the per-frame scores, and the file records the vote (`frames`, `frames_lowered`).

**The margin (20 percent) and the drop distance (8 px).**

- *Margin.* Where least squares is right, the fitted skyline scores within 0.12 percent of the climb in every recovery scene (0.002 to 0.1 percent on a clean frame, 0.12 beside a ghost edge, 0.016 against a terrain model 0.1 degree off), and 0.14 percent on the real WHTM1 frames at the committed position. The one scene the margin must not catch is the one the narrowing bands exist to repair: one wide pass with a cloud held in every frame loses 11.5 percent per frame, the three-band fit loses nothing, and a guard that rescued the one-pass fit would leave no scene that needs the bands (`test_a_cloud_in_every_frame_is_dropped_by_the_narrower_trace_bands` runs that comparison and must keep seeing the one-pass fit follow the cloud). What the guard must catch loses 33 to 44 percent. 20 percent is the geometric midpoint (19.5) of the last two, about 0.2 degrees of azimuth or 3 percent of the field of view on that lens. The first draft used 10 percent, which refused the one-pass scene and broke that test.
- *Drop distance.* 8 px is four robust scales (`f_scale` of 2 px): the soft-L1 loss is already linear there, so least squares itself discounts such a column, and a cloud base 15 to 25 rows above the ridge is two to three times farther. The count in the file is the number of traced columns left out of any of the refit's three passes. The wide pass sees the cloud and the narrow ones, centred on the ridge, do not, so the reported residual of a refit still spans the whole frame. The refit keeps the columns that a slightly wrong terrain model only misplaces (a pixel or two): in the scene rendered from a ridge 0.1 degree off, the refit drops the cloud's 204 columns and ends where the fit ends without the cloud.

**Rejected alternative: reject trace segments whose edge is not continuous across frames.** A cloud moves between frames and the ridge does not, so fitting only on columns where the frames agree would drop a moving cloud. The failure here is a cloud edge held in every frame, and a slow or stationary cloud bank defeats any test built on motion: the edge-energy comparison does not depend on the cloud moving. (The synthetic scene holds the cloud at identical rows in every frame, the hardest case for the rejected alternative and no harder for this one.) Also rejected: schema 3 with a migration, which would rewrite the frozen WHTM1 fit and change the digest the observations cite; and a guard on the mean map with a larger margin, which no margin makes work (above).

**The record.** `least_squares_guard` holds `climbed_energy`, `fitted_energy`, `refit_energy` (null when no refit was made), `margin`, `frames`, `frames_lowered`, `refused`, `columns_dropped` and `outcome` (`accepted`, `refit` or `kept_climb`). `write_fit` writes it whenever the fit carries one, which `calibrate_camera` always supplies. `CameraFit.from_json` accepts a file without it (fitted before the guard existed) and validates it when present: it refuses a null object, an unknown outcome, a `refused` flag that disagrees with the outcome or with the vote, a negative or non-finite energy, a refit energy where no refit was made, and columns dropped where nothing was refused. A fit without a record serialises without one, so re-writing the committed WHTM1 reproduces it byte for byte (`test_the_committed_whtm1_fit_predates_the_guard_and_is_byte_for_byte_unchanged`).

**What broke and what it taught.**

- The first draft followed the spec literally (mean map) and failed the haze test; see above. The vote by frame is the smallest change that keeps that test and catches the capture.
- The first margin (10 percent) refused the one-pass variant of the bands scene and silently turned a test about the bands into a test of the guard. Anyone who lowers the margin below about 12 percent will meet `test_a_cloud_in_every_frame_is_dropped_by_the_narrower_trace_bands` first.
- The first count of dropped columns was the last pass's, which is zero: by then the band is narrow and centred on the ridge. It is now the count over all three passes.
- A capture that costs less than 20 percent of the edge score (a stretch of 100 columns or fewer, or a cloud close to the ridge) is not refused. The scores are in the file, so the loss can be read off the record: `fitted_energy` against `climbed_energy`.
- The `fit` command does not print the guard's verdict; it is in the file. A one-line summary is a small follow-up in `lastlight.commands.camgeom`, outside this slice's files.
- The new tests were attacked by hand with 29 mutants of the guard and its record (margin too small or too large, drop distance huge or tiny, any frame or all frames refusing, the refit always or never accepted, the mean map, an absolute margin, the last pass's count, the wrong side of the distance, the refit started from the fitted orientation, each check of the record removed). Four survived the first draft of the tests and were killed by the scenes and cases added since: a drop distance of 0.2 px (the clean scene's ridge sits within it; the mismatched-terrain scene does not), one frame refusing and all frames needed (the haze scene's vote, and a third frame that shows no ridge), and an outcome that disagrees with a `refused` flag the vote agrees with.
