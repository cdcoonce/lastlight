# Camera geometry: pixel to sky coordinates from the skyline

**Decided:** `lastlight.camgeom` maps every pixel of a public camera to a sky direction (issue #16). A pinhole `CameraModel` gives `pixel_to_sky` and `sky_to_pixel`; the orientation (azimuth, elevation, roll, field of view) is fitted by matching the skyline in the camera's frames to the horizon a public DEM predicts; the sun is an independent check; the result is frozen in `src/lastlight/cameras/geometry/WHTM1.json`; and `lastlight camgeom fit`, `mask` and `holdout` drive it. **A fit is `provisional` until the sun check passes**, and says in its file how far the pointing and pixel scale could be off.

**The real result (WHTM1, five frames fitted on, three held out), refrozen after the adversarial review of #22:**

- Azimuth 289.3, elevation 1.51, roll 0.34, horizontal field of view 17.9 degrees. Skyline residual **1.60 px RMS (0.036 degrees), median 1.13 px**, over all 800 columns. The issue's target was under 2 px. (The first freeze, from a coarser position search, was azimuth 288.7, elevation 1.66, field of view 19.4, 1.65 px.)
- **Held out.** `lastlight camgeom holdout --camera WHTM1 --frames <archive>` lays the frozen skyline on the three frames the fit never saw (taken after the sun had set below the fit's cut-off, at -0.6, -3.8 and -6.8 degrees) and traces each frame's own skyline within 12 rows of it: **1.55, 1.56 and 1.54 px RMS (0.035 degrees)**. These replace the 1.63, 1.65 and 2.01 px of the first freeze, which came from a calculation that was never committed and that the reviewer could not reproduce for the third frame. The number depends on the band: at 30 rows the third frame is lost to another edge (12.6 px RMS, median 1.46 px); at 6 rows nothing changes. The default band is 12 rows, the fit's own last band, so a held-out frame is judged the way a fitted one is. The check shows that the frozen curve follows the ridge in other frames of the same evening. It does not show the pixel scale, which is the weak part (next bullet).
- **Sun check: skipped, and it will stay skipped until spring.** With an 18 degree lens pointed at 289, the sun (azimuth 251 to 258 on this evening) is about 30 degrees outside the frame in all five frames. The sun crosses this frame at sunset from about April to early September.
- **The field of view is not pinned down.** Position, field of view and azimuth are not separately identified. A 1 km grid over the whole 8 km box (289 fits) puts the best at the box's east edge (8 km east, 1 km north of the site file's point). 36 positions fit within 10 percent of it, with azimuth 283.3 to 291.8, field of view 17.7 to 28.5 degrees and a pixel scale of 27.5 to 44.9 px per degree; the best fit sits at the fine end of that range (44.2 px per degree, 0.023 degrees per row), so a row may be up to 60 percent more degrees than the fit says. The first freeze's 2 km grid had called its floor (6 km east, 2 km north) "not on the edge of the box"; the finer grid shows that was a grid artefact. A ring of 72 probes one kilometre outside the box fits marginally better still (1.59 px against 1.60, which is inside the 3 percent roughness between neighbouring positions), and the reviewer's extension of the scan to 20 km east reached 1.47 px with a 12.8 degree lens: the valley's floor is outside the box, and the field of view is limited by the position prior, not by the data. The ranges above are therefore lower bounds, and the file says so (`best_on_box_edge`, `residual_falls_beyond_box`, `uncertainty.lower_bounds`).
- **Azimuth against the operator's.** The fit lands 15.3 degrees from the registry's `view_azimuth_deg` of 274, which is 85 percent of the frame's width, and no near-best fit has an azimuth below 283.3. Within 4 degrees of 274 the reviewer's best anywhere in an extended window cost about 35 percent more residual (2.2 px). The fitted axis is credible as the best fit at the adopted position; it is not an independent confirmation of the operator's direction, and the file now records `stated_azimuth_deg` and `azimuth_minus_stated_deg` so the gap is on the record.

**How the fit works, and why it has three stages:**

- **A coarse search on edge strength, then a trace, then least squares.** The first version traced the strongest edge in every column and fitted that. On real frames the strongest edges are cloud bottoms and the foreground, not the ridge. So the coarse stage scores each candidate by the edge strength the predicted curve passes through (no skyline is chosen yet), the best candidates are climbed on that score, the best of those is traced in a band around its prediction by a dynamic program that keeps the path continuous, and a robust (soft L1) least-squares fit moves azimuth, elevation, roll and field of view onto the median of the frames' traces, three times, with a narrower band each time (30, 18 and 12 rows).
- **Sky brighter than terrain is the only edge that counts.** `edge_strength` is the drop in brightness going down; a cloud's bottom or a white stamp's top never scores.
- **The timestamp is excluded, and the box is widened.** The smoothing and the comparison spread a bright stamp's edge a few rows beyond its rectangle, so an excluded box is widened by their reach. Without that, the path rode the edge just below the box.
- **Horizon from the terrain, with curvature built in.** For each azimuth, the largest angle `atan2((R + H) cos(s/R) - (R + h), (R + H) sin(s/R))` over a ray sampled every 30 m from 2 to 50 km; terrain nearer than 2 km (houses and trees beside the mast) is skipped. A synthetic cone matches the analytic elevation within 0.05 degrees; a level plain gives the geometric dip; and a test shows the flat-Earth answer would be 0.12 degrees off, so the cone test cannot pass without curvature. Refraction is not modelled: it lifts a ridge 50 km away by about 0.03 degrees (coefficient 0.13, k s / 2R) and a nearer one by less. (The first version of this log said under 0.02.)

**What each stage is for, and the scene that needs it** (review finding B1). The first tests rendered the scene from the very horizon the fit was given, so every stage recovered the truth alone, and the tolerances were 200 times looser than the fit's accuracy. The recovery tolerances are now 0.02 degrees, 0.1 percent of the field of view and 0.15 px, ten times what the fit achieves on a clean scene, and each stage below has a scene built so that it has a job the others cannot do (`tests/test_camgeom_orientation.py`, `tests/test_camgeom_skyline.py`; each scene first runs the fit without the stage to show the job is real). The rule for deleting a stage was: no realistic scene needs it and it changes the real WHTM1 result by under 2 percent RMS. Every stage has a scene, so **all are pinned and none deleted**:

- **Stage 2, the climb on edge energy, decides the answer.** On the real frames, without it 10 of the 25 positions of a 4 km grid over the box end in another basin (field of view up to 2.5 times different); at the first freeze's position it landed at azimuth 275.1 with a 53.7 degree lens and 3.54 px, as the reviewer saw. (At the frozen position the coarse top candidate happens to be right, and the result is unchanged.) Pinned by a ridge that repeats every 5.5 degrees and a true 21.5 degree lens that falls between two of the coarse grid's field-of-view steps: the coarse search ranks first an alignment two periods to the right with a 6 percent wider lens, and the fit without the climb ends 11.0 degrees off at 6.9 px; with it, 0.0003 degrees and 0.06 px.
- **Stage 3, robust least squares on the median trace: polish on WHTM1, decisive in haze.** Skipping it moves the real result by 0.02 degrees and 0.6 percent of the RMS (1.610 px against 1.600). It matters when one frame holds an edge that out-shouts a faint ridge: in haze (30 percent of the contrast) a cloud's dark underside in one of three frames is nine times stronger than the ridge, the climb on the mean edge map settles 0.28 degrees low in elevation and 0.4 percent off in field of view (8 px RMS), and the median of the per-frame traces outvotes the cloud. Skipping the stage, keeping the climbed field of view, and keeping the climbed azimuth and elevation are each caught by that scene.
- **The narrowing bands (30, 18, 12 rows) decide the reported residual.** On WHTM1 a single wide pass gives the same answer. Pinned by a cloud base 22 rows above the ridge in all three frames over 160 columns: one pass follows the cloud (RMS 9.7 px, azimuth 0.09 degrees off), the three passes drop it (0.05 px, 0.001 degrees).
- **Trace continuity: the step limit (2 rows between columns) is load-bearing, the cost per row moved is a tie-breaker.** Without the step limit, fourteen 3-column bright fragments 8 to 12 rows above the ridge pull the path off it (13 px at worst, RMS 2.5 px against 0.12). The cost per row moved changes WHTM1 by 0.1 percent of the RMS (1.599 against 1.600) and no rendered scene needs it short of a 6-column fragment; it is pinned by a hand-built edge map (a sample 6 percent stronger and two rows off does not buy a detour). Both are kept.
- **A finding the new scenes exposed, not fixed here.** Stage 3 can be captured where the climb is not: with a bright cloud edge held in all three frames 15 to 25 rows above the ridge over a stretch near the edge of the frame, the per-frame traces and their median all follow the cloud, and the least-squares stage moves the camera by a degree or more (azimuth 1.1, roll 1.8, field of view 6 percent) while the climb alone was right. The WHTM1 frames do not do this. A guard that keeps the climbed fit when the traced fit lowers the edge energy is the obvious cheap fix; it deserves a slice of its own.

**The first answer was wrong, and the data said so.** At the described site the fit settled at a 72 degree lens with 3.6 px RMS, and the leftmost 100 columns lay 10 px below the prediction: the observed ridge there sat at an elevation of -1.4 degrees, lower than any horizon can be for a camera a few metres above flat ground. A radial term would have absorbed it (2.8 px) but pegged at its bound and stretched the lens by 17 percent: a sign that the lens model, not the lens, was wrong. A scan of positions with the field-of-view range widened to 10 to 120 degrees found a long valley of fits at 1.6 to 1.9 px with 15 to 25 degree lenses, positions a few kilometres north and east, and a median residual near 1 px. A narrow lens is what a visibility camera is for. **What this does not show** (review finding N2) is that a wide lens cannot fit at the site. The 72 degree solution is the local minimum the search settled in, not the best at the site: a dense multi-start at the site (the reviewer's) reaches 2.8 px with azimuth 266 and a 17 degree lens, and the south-west half of the box is filled with the same 3.3 to 3.6 px plateau at lenses of 70 to 100 degrees. The site is still worse than the valley (2.8 px against 1.6), so positions a few kilometres east are preferred, but `rms_px_at_site` in the file (3.63 px) is the pipeline's result at the site, not a floor. The search that is now part of `fit` (`--position-box-km`) is that scan made repeatable: a fine grid (1 km) over the whole stated box, fitting from scratch at every point, never choosing a fit pinned to a field-of-view limit, and then a ring of probes one step outside the box that records whether the residual keeps falling. It takes 361 fits, 10 to 12 minutes.

**Position stays out of the repository.** The privacy scanner (P003) flags any coordinate pair in Arizona except the reference point, and the issue's own plan to commit a position per camera would trip it. `lastlight camgeom fit` reads position and view direction from a private site file (`--site-file` or `LASTLIGHT_CAMERA_SITES`); the frozen fit holds orientation only, and the position search records its result as an offset in kilometres from that file's point. `CameraModel` keeps `lat`, `lon` and `elev_m` as the issue specifies, and `CameraFit.camera_model(lat, lon, elev_m)` takes them from the caller; the pixel-to-sky mapping does not depend on them.

**Provisional fits (schema 2).** The file carries `provisional` (true exactly when the sun check has not passed; loading refuses a file where the two disagree), an `uncertainty` block with `azimuth_deg`, `hfov_deg` and `px_per_deg` ranges across the nearly equal fits and whether they are lower bounds, and the registry's `stated_azimuth_deg` with `azimuth_minus_stated_deg`. Loading is as strict as `CameraModel`: a field of view of zero or 180, a zero image size, NaN anywhere, and repeated or unsorted skyline columns are refused with the field named. `fit` exits 1, after writing the file so it can be inspected, when the sun check fails; a skipped check exits 0 with `provisional` true. A frame that cannot be decoded, a DEM tile that cannot be read, a cached tile that is not a TIFF or is a stub (discarded and fetched again), a download that is not a tile, and an output path that cannot be written each give one line and exit 2. For #17: refuse a provisional fit unless the caller passes an explicit override, specify contrast profiles as bands of elevation above the local ridge within about 2 to 5 degrees (the frame covers about -2 to +5 degrees, so the design's "2 degrees above the horizon against 20 degrees" cannot be built from this camera), and carry the pixel-scale range with them.

**Deviations from the issue and from `docs/design.md`:**

- The issue asks for the skyline of a clear daytime frame. The archive held no clear frame (five cloudy frames from the late afternoon). The fit therefore uses every frame with the sun at or above the horizon, takes the median of their traces, and says how many frames it used.
- `docs/design.md` said geometry is "calibrated from the sun's track". The issue makes skyline matching primary and the sun an independent check, which is better (a fit input cannot also be a test) and is now what the design says.
- The position is searched, not fixed. The issue allows it ("within a stated prior box"); the box is 8 km because the operator says "Avondale", which is not a point. The box stays the prior; it was not widened to chase the valley.

**Rejected alternatives:** a radial term in the model (not identified by this data, the diagnostic is recorded instead, see `radial_term` in the fit file); streaming the DEM with GDAL's `/vsicurl/` (a one-time 40 MB download to a cache is simpler and makes every later fit offline); a hand-written TIFF reader (rasterio reads the Copernicus tiles and merges neighbours); scipy's optimiser replaced by a hand-rolled one (the robust loss is the point); fitting the sun as an input; a coarse grid followed by a fine one around the best coarse point (it hid that the best sat on the box's edge, and a ring with probes every 4 km missed dips that a ring with probes every kilometre found).

**What broke:**

- Bilinear sampling beside a no-data hole returned NaN even at a node with full weight, because `0 * NaN` is NaN. Corners with no weight are now dropped from the sum.
- A DEM window ending exactly at the camera's longitude left the camera on the edge, outside the posts that were read. The window is padded, and the tiles are named from the unpadded box: padding by 2 km first pulled in a whole neighbouring 40 MB tile for the last kilometre of one ray. The window now also reaches the ring of probes.
- A position search that scored the best residual alone chose fits with the field of view pinned at its limit; those are excluded and counted.
- The fit file's summary held NumPy integers and booleans, which JSON refuses; the command test that runs the search caught it.
- The repository's own privacy scanner flagged this slice's code: a field-of-view range whose two limits fell inside the latitude and longitude boxes read as a coordinate pair (the range is now 10 to 120), literal grid corners in the DEM tests, and a docstring that spelled a time of day. Tests now derive positions from the reference point, and the fit tests use the scanner's own rules as their probe.
- **The trace crashed with an `IndexError` when the expected skyline lay wholly outside the frame** for some column (a candidate placement that predicts the ridge below the last row). The new scenes found it on the way to something else; a 289-fit position search would have met it at some corner of the box. Those columns are now NaN and do not vote.

**Next:** observe (issue #17) builds on `CameraFit` and `sky_mask`, and should treat WHTM1 as provisional (above). Refit WHTM1 when clear-day frames exist and again when the sun enters the frame (April to early September), and freeze that fit before the first scored evening. If the position of the camera becomes known, replace the box with it: that, not more frames of this evening, is what pins the field of view.
